# Pocket-Web-Studio User Guide

Pocket-Web-Studio lets you build a website by dragging things onto a page and typing on it. You never need to write code: it writes the HTML, CSS and JavaScript for you, and the result works on computers, tablets and phones.

This guide is for version 2.1.

## 1. Installing

Download the installer for your computer from the project's **Releases** page on GitHub:

| Computer | File to download |
| --- | --- |
| Windows | `Pocket-Web-Studio Setup 2.1.0.exe` |
| Linux | the `.AppImage` (runs on most Linux systems) or the `.deb` (Ubuntu, Debian, Mint) |
| Mac | the `.dmg` |

**Windows:** double-click the setup file. Because the app isn't code-signed, Windows may show "Windows protected your PC". Click **More info**, then **Run anyway**.

**Mac:** open the `.dmg` and drag Pocket-Web-Studio into Applications. The first time, right-click the app and choose **Open**, then **Open** again.

**Linux AppImage:** right-click the file, open **Properties › Permissions**, tick **Allow executing file as program**, then double-click it.

## 2. Your first website

When Pocket-Web-Studio opens, it asks what you'd like to start with:

- **Blank website** – one empty page with a header and footer.
- **Small business** – Home, About and Contact pages for a shop, café or local service.
- **Portfolio** – a dark, picture-led site for photographers, artists and makers.

Pick one. The starter websites are full of sample words and pictures, so you can see how things work and simply replace them with your own.

Then:

1. Click any words on the page and type your own.
2. Click a picture and choose **Change picture** in the panel on the right, or drag a photo from your computer onto it.
3. Press **Ctrl+S** to save your project.
4. When you're happy, click **Publish** (top right).

## 3. Finding your way around

```
┌───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ Top bar: New · Open · Import · Save · Undo/Redo · Edit/Preview/Code   │
│          Computer/Tablet/Phone · Open in browser · Publish            │
├──────────────┬───────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────┤
│ Left panel   │                                       │ Right panel    │
│  Add         │          Your page (the canvas)       │ Settings for   │
│  Pages       │                                       │ whatever you   │
│  Design      │                                       │ have selected  │
└──────────────┴───────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────┘
```

- **Left panel, Add tab:** elements and ready-made sections to drag onto your page.
- **Left panel, Pages tab:** your pages. Add, rename, reorder and duplicate them here.
- **Left panel, Design tab:** colours, fonts, corner style and website settings (name, language, site icon, website address and spelling).
- **The canvas:** your page, exactly as visitors will see it. Click anything to select it.
- **Right panel:** the settings for what's selected. Nothing selected? It shows the page's settings.
- **The small toolbar** that appears above a selected item lets you move it up or down, duplicate it, delete it, or drag it by its grip.

**Undo and Redo** (Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y) work for everything, so feel free to experiment.

## 4. Adding things to a page

There are two ways:

- **Drag** an element or section from the Add tab onto the page. A blue line shows exactly where it will land.
- **Click** an element or section in the Add tab. It's added just after whatever you have selected.

You can also drag photos straight from your computer's folders onto the page.

---

## 5. Changing text

Click any text on the page and start typing. This works for headings, text boxes, buttons, captions, quotes, table cells, questions, the logo text and the footer.

In a **text box**, select some words to get the formatting toolbar: **bold**, *italic*, bullet list, numbered list and link.

---

## 6. Moving and resizing things

### Moving

- **Press and drag** anything on the page (a picture, a card, a heading) to move it. A blue line shows where it will land. You can drop it in another column or another section.
- To move a **whole section**, press on its empty edge (the space around its contents) and drag.
- The page scrolls by itself when you drag near the top or bottom.
- Press **Esc** while dragging to cancel.
- Inside a text box that's already selected, dragging selects words instead of moving the box. Click outside it first if you want to move it.
- The **up and down arrows** on the small toolbar also move an item.

### Resizing

- Select a picture, card, gallery, table, text box, quote, questions list and more. A **round handle** appears on its edge. Drag it to set the width. A label shows the width as a percentage of the column.
- Or use the **Width** slider in the right panel, with quick buttons for **¼**, **½**, **¾** and **Full**.
- Use **Align** to place a narrower item on the left, in the middle or on the right.
- For a **Space** element, drag the handle at the bottom to set its height, or use the **Exact height** slider.
- On phones, items always use the full width so they stay readable.

## 7. The elements

| Element | What it's for |
| --- | --- |
| Heading | Titles. Use H1 once per page for the main title, H2 for sections, H3/H4 below them. Can be a link. |
| Text box | Paragraphs, with bold, italic, lists and links. |
| Button | A call to action that links somewhere. |
| Link | A simple text link, with an optional arrow. |
| Picture | A photo or image, with a caption, a description, a shape and an optional link. |
| Line | A divider between parts of a page. |
| Space | Empty space of a height you choose. |
| Gallery | A grid of pictures that open larger when clicked. |
| Slideshow | Pictures that change by themselves, with an optional slow zoom. |
| Video | YouTube, Vimeo or your own video file. |
| Map | A Google map of an address. |
| Scrolling text | A banner of words that scrolls across the page. |
| Card | A picture, heading, text and button together, for services, products or team members. |
| Quote | A testimonial or quotation with the person's name. |
| Table | Rows and columns of information. |
| Questions | Questions and answers that open when clicked (FAQs). |
| Contact form | A form visitors fill in to contact you. See [Contact forms](#14-contact-forms). |
| Own code | Your own HTML, such as a booking widget someone gave you. Pictures you've added can be used in it. |

## 8. Sections and columns

Every page is built from **sections** stacked from top to bottom. Each section has **columns**: 1, 2 (equal or uneven), 3 or 4.

**Ready-made sections** in the Add tab include a big banner, picture and text, three cards, gallery, full-width slideshow, testimonial, call to action, questions, contact, scrolling banner, video, and empty 1, 2 or 3-column layouts.

Click a section's empty edge to select it. In the right panel you can change:

- the column layout,
- the space above and below, the width, and how contents line up vertically,
- the background colour or a background picture, with a dark overlay to keep text readable,
- **parallax** (the background stays still while the page scrolls),
- a **section name**, so links can jump straight to this section.

On phones, columns stack on top of each other automatically.

## 9. Pictures and video

- **Adding pictures:** drag photos from your computer onto the page, onto a picture, into a gallery or into a slideshow. Or select a picture and click **Change picture**.
- **Automatic resizing:** big camera photos are shrunk and compressed (to the WebP format) so your website stays fast. A 4 MB photo usually becomes a few hundred KB.
- **Describe the picture:** always fill this in. Screen readers read it out for blind visitors, and search engines use it. Publish reminds you if one is missing.
- **Shape:** crop a picture to Square, Landscape, Portrait, Wide or Circle. **Original** keeps it as taken. Cards have their own picture shape setting.
- **Video:** paste a YouTube or Vimeo link, or choose a video file from your computer.

## 10. Links

You can link from:

- a **Link** element,
- a **heading**, **button**, **picture** or **card** (the "Goes to" or "Make the heading a link to" setting),
- **words in a text box**: select them and press **Ctrl+K** or the link button.

A link can go to:

- another of your pages,
- a section of a page (give the section a name first),
- a web address,
- an email address (opens the visitor's email app),
- a phone number (rings it on a phone).

Tick **Open in a new tab** for links to other websites if you like.

## 11. Pages and the menu

Open the **Pages** tab on the left.

- **Add a page** with the button at the top, or press Ctrl+Shift+N.
- **Rename, reorder, duplicate or delete** pages there. The first page is your home page.
- The menu in the header and the links in the footer update themselves.

Click a page to see its settings on the right:

- **Page name**: what appears in the menu.
- **Browser tab title** and **Description for search engines**: what Google shows. Keep the description under about 160 characters.
- **Show this page in the menu**: switch off for pages you only link to.
- **Place in the menu**: choose **In the drop-down under "…"** to put this page in a drop-down list under another page. Pages placed this way are indented in the Pages tab.

### Drop-down menus

1. Make sure the parent page (for example *Services*) is shown at the top level of the menu.
2. Click each page that should sit under it, and set **Place in the menu** to *In the drop-down under "Services"*.

Drop-downs open when visitors hover, and when they use the keyboard. On phones they're listed underneath.

### Extra menu links

To add links that aren't your own pages (a shop, a booking site, social media), click the header, then go to **Extra menu links › Add a menu link**. Each link can sit at the top level or in a drop-down under one of your pages.

## 12. Colours, fonts and the header and footer

Open the **Design** tab.

- **Colour scheme:** pick a ready-made scheme, or set your own five colours (main, accent, page background, card background and text). Every colour picker offers your scheme first, so changing the scheme changes the whole site together.
- **Fonts:** choose one of nine font pairings, or mix heading and body fonts from 19 choices.
- **Corners:** square, soft or round.
- **Website:** name, language, site icon (the little picture in the browser tab), website address and spelling.

**Header:** click it on the page. Choose logo text or a logo picture, logo on the left or centred, whether it stays at the top while scrolling, and its colours.

**Footer:** click it on the page. Type your footer text. `{year}` is replaced with the current year automatically. You can also show or hide the page links.

Each element also has a **Look** section in the right panel: alignment, width, text size, colours, padding, rounded corners, shadow, and **Hide on phones**.

---

## 13. Movement and effects

In an element's **Movement** settings:

- **Appear when scrolled to:** fade, rise, slide in from either side, or zoom.
- **Keep moving:** float, pulse, sway or spin (good for small pictures and icons).

Slideshows can slowly zoom, the scrolling text element scrolls, and section backgrounds can use parallax.

Visitors who have asked their device to reduce motion won't see any of these effects, for their comfort.

## 14. Contact forms

A website can't send email by itself, so the form gives you two choices under **Send messages to**:

- **My email:** the visitor's own email app opens with their message filled in. Easy, but not every visitor has an email app set up.
- **A form service:** free services such as Formspree, Basin or Netlify Forms give you an address starting `https://`. Paste it in and they'll email each message to you. This is the smoother option.

Tick the fields you want (name, email, phone, subject, message) and set the button text.

## 15. Checking your spelling

- Spelling mistakes are **underlined in red** as you type.
- **Right-click** an underlined word for suggestions, or choose **Add to dictionary**.
- To check **every page at once**, press **F7** or go to **Design › Website › Check spelling on every page**. For each word you can:
  - choose a suggestion (or type your own) and click **Change all**,
  - **Ignore** it for this website,
  - **Add to dictionary** for all your websites,
  - **Show me** where it is.
- The dictionary follows your website's **language** (Design › Website).
- To turn the red underlines off, use **View › Underline Spelling Mistakes**.

On Windows and Mac the computer's own spell checker is used. On Linux the dictionary downloads the first time it's needed.

## 16. Previewing your website

- **Preview** (top bar, or Ctrl+2) shows your site with every effect working: menus, slideshows, galleries and movement. Click links to move between pages.
- The **Computer / Tablet / Phone** buttons show your page at those sizes.
- **Open in browser** (F12) opens it in your normal web browser.
- **Code** (Ctrl+3) shows the code Pocket-Web-Studio has written for the current page. It's read-only.
- **Edit** (Ctrl+1) takes you back to editing.

---

## 17. Saving and opening

- **Save** (Ctrl+S) stores your whole website, including pictures, in one `.pocketweb` file. Keep it somewhere safe and back it up.
- **Save As** (Ctrl+Shift+S) saves a copy under a new name.
- **Open** (Ctrl+O), or double-click a `.pocketweb` file.
- Older `.ezyweb` and `.weaver` files still open.
- If you try to close with unsaved changes, Pocket-Web-Studio asks first.

The `.pocketweb` file is your editable project. The **published website** (next sections) is a separate folder of web files.

## 18. Importing an existing website

Already have a website as a folder of files? Click **Import** (Ctrl+I) and choose the folder that holds its `index.html`.

Pocket-Web-Studio rebuilds it as editable pages: sections, columns, headings, text, pictures, galleries, buttons, tables, quotes, questions, videos and maps, plus the site's colours, fonts, logo, menu order and footer. Anything it can't turn into an element (such as a custom widget) is kept as **Own code** so nothing is lost.

A report shows what was imported. Your original files are never changed.

Import works best with simple, hand-made or exported websites. Websites built by online builders that need their own servers (for example Wix) can't be imported this way.

## 19. Publishing your website

Click **Publish** (Ctrl+P).

1. A **check** runs first and lists anything worth fixing: missing picture descriptions, buttons that go nowhere, forms with no address, and whether a website address is set. Click **Show me** to jump to each one.
2. Choose a folder. Pocket-Web-Studio writes your finished website there:

```
index.html, about.html, …   your pages
css/style.css               the design
js/site.js                  only if your site needs it
images/ and media/          your pictures and videos
sitemap.xml, robots.txt     when a website address is set
CNAME                       when the host is GitHub Pages
```

3. Upload the **contents** of that folder to a web host.

Publishing again to the same folder updates it. Other files in the folder are left alone.

### Free places to host your website

- **Netlify Drop:** go to app.netlify.com/drop and drag your published folder onto the page. That's it.
- **Cloudflare Pages:** in the Cloudflare dashboard, create a Pages project and upload the folder.
- **GitHub Pages** (using GitHub Desktop):
  1. In GitHub Desktop, choose **File › New repository**, name it, and create it.
  2. Copy the contents of your published folder into the repository folder (**Repository › Show in Explorer**).
  3. Type a summary, click **Commit to main**, then **Publish repository**. Untick "Keep this code private" if your account needs a public repository for Pages.
  4. On GitHub.com, open the repository, go to **Settings › Pages**, set **Source** to *Deploy from a branch*, choose **main** and **/ (root)**, then **Save**.
  5. After a minute or two your site appears at `https://your-name.github.io/repository-name/`.
  6. To update it later, publish into the same folder, then commit and push in GitHub Desktop.

## 20. Your own domain name and free HTTPS

A domain name is your website's address, like `harbourlanebakery.co.uk`. You rent one for roughly £5–£15 a year from a domain company such as Cloudflare, Porkbun, Namecheap, IONOS or 123-reg.

Open the guide from **Design › Website › Website address › Set up…**, from **Help › Domain Name & Free HTTPS Setup**, or from the Publish window. It has four steps:

1. **Address:** type your domain. Include `www.` if that's how you want it shown.
2. **Host:** choose GitHub Pages, Netlify, Cloudflare Pages, or your own server or another host, and fill in your user name, site name or server address.
3. **DNS records:** the exact records to add at your domain company, each with a **Copy** button, plus where to click at your host. Log in to your domain company, open the domain's DNS settings, delete any old records for the same name, and add these.
   - **Check my domain** looks up your records and tells you whether they point the right way. Changes usually work within an hour but can take up to 48 hours.
4. **HTTPS:** how to get the free padlock certificate.
   - **GitHub Pages:** tick **Enforce HTTPS** in Settings › Pages once it's available.
   - **Netlify** and **Cloudflare Pages:** automatic.
   - **Your own server:** ready-to-copy settings for Caddy (which handles certificates itself) or Certbot commands for nginx or Apache, with your domain already filled in.
   - **Hosting company with a control panel:** look for SSL/TLS, AutoSSL or Let's Encrypt and switch it on.

Click **Save and finish**, then **publish again**. Your website now gets a `sitemap.xml`, `robots.txt`, links that search engines prefer and, for GitHub Pages, a `CNAME` file.

## 21. The browser version

There is also a version that runs in a web browser, handy for trying Pocket-Web-Studio out. Editing works the same way, with these differences:

- Your work is kept in that browser only. **Save** stores it there; it can't save or open `.pocketweb` files. Very large pictures may not fit, and clearing your browser's data removes your work. Use the desktop app for real projects.
- **Publish** can't write a folder. It checks your site, then you can open the **Code** view to copy each file.
- **Import** works: choose the website's folder when your browser asks. **Open in browser** just shows the Preview.
- Spelling uses your browser's own checker: right-click an underlined word. Checking every page at once needs the desktop app.
- **Check my domain** needs the desktop app.

## 23. Troubleshooting

**Windows says "Windows protected your PC".**
The app isn't code-signed. Click **More info › Run anyway**.

**I can't move a text box by dragging it.**
When a text box is selected, dragging inside it selects words. Click somewhere else first, then drag it, or use its grip on the small toolbar.

**No red spelling underlines.**
Check **View › Underline Spelling Mistakes** is ticked and the website's language is right (Design › Website).

**"Check my domain" says my records are wrong.**
If you've just changed them, wait an hour and check again. Make sure you deleted old A or AAAA records for the same name.

**HTTPS isn't available yet on GitHub Pages.**
GitHub can take up to 24 hours after your DNS records work.

**My contact form opens an email program instead of sending.**
That's the **My email** option. Switch to **A form service** for a smoother experience.

**Something went wrong.**
Press Ctrl+Z to undo. Your saved `.pocketweb` file isn't changed until you save again.
